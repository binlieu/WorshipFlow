## Decisions

- **Source compatibility.** Everything added is a new type, a new member or a defaulted parameter. `scene(for:state:theme:look:props:clockText:)` stays callable as before (`media` sits before `clockText` with a default; all arguments are labelled, so no existing call changes). `encode(...)`, `renderImage(...)`, `WindowOutput.init` and `DisplayOutput.init` gained defaulted parameters at the end.
- **Transport.** `MediaTransport` holds `phase` (idle, running, paused), a base position and an anchor; `ended` is derived from the clock (running, not looping, duration reached), as `ShowTimer` derives its values. Duration 0 (still, or length not known) never ends. Pause on an ended clip leaves it ended. Seek on an ended clip plays from the new position (the phase is still running). Play on an ended clip, or from the very end, restarts at zero. A looping seek to the duration lands on 0. Non-finite times are ignored.
- **Layers.** Audio is ignored by `setBackground` / `startForeground` and leaves the current layer unchanged. A background always loops and starts playing when set; a foreground plays once.
- **Scene rules (tests plus two judgement calls).**
  - Background media shows only when the theme background is `.media` and the Look shows the background layer. `showsVideo` stays true for a video and becomes false for an image (so the theme's shared loop does not show under a still).
  - Foreground media is shown on audience and broadcast, **only when the Look shows the slide layer** (my addition: a broadcast screen set to props-only must not suddenly get a full-frame clip). It hides the slide text, keeps props. The stage never shows media. A stopped show has no media; Clear keeps it.
- **Transport clock.** `MediaClock.now()` is `ProcessInfo.systemUptime` (monotonic). The operator layer should use it for `MediaLayers` times and for `MediaRegistry.apply(_:at:)` (default argument).
- **Registry and the package graph.** `OutputEngine` does not depend on `MediaEngine` (Package.swift is fixed), so the output-facing contract lives in OutputEngine: `protocol MediaFrameSource { func currentPixelBuffer(for: SceneMedia, layer: MediaLayerSlot) -> CVPixelBuffer? }`. `MediaRegistry` has exactly that method, so the app adopts it with `extension MediaRegistry: MediaFrameSource {}` (same pattern as `LoopingVideoSource: VideoSourceFactory` in `App/Sources/VideoSupport.swift`). **I did not add that line (App is the other agent's).** Pass the registry as `mediaSource:` to `WindowOutput` / `DisplayOutput`; call `registry.register(asset)` for each asset and `registry.apply(layers)` after every `MediaLayers` change.
- **Everything is a BGRA pixel buffer.** Video frames come from `AVPlayerItemVideoOutput`; a still is decoded once (ImageIO, premultiplied sRGB BGRA, longest side 4096) into a Metal-compatible `CVPixelBuffer` on a utility queue. Outputs convert with their own per-thread `CVMetalTextureCache`. The render side is a pure lookup: it never opens a file, never decodes, never waits. A still that is still decoding gives no frame for a few ticks.
- **Registry shape.** Two slots (background, foreground), each with at most one open source, so the same asset can be both. `apply` opens newly chosen media, closes dropped media and hands each `VideoPlayerSource` its `MediaTransport`. Background video is opened looping and muted; foreground video plays once with audio on (default output). Stills are LRU-bounded (default 8, a still held by a layer is never dropped). Missing or undecodable media goes into `unavailableAssetIDs`, nothing throws.
- **`VideoPlayerSource`.** `AVPlayer` (non-looping, `actionAtItemEnd = .pause` so the last frame stays) or `AVQueuePlayer` plus `AVPlayerLooper` (looping). It follows a `MediaTransport` (`apply(_:at:)`, or `play/pause/seek/stop`). End of a non-looping clip is reported by the player's did-play-to-end notification (`setOnEnded`, may fire on any thread) and `status()` also reports `.ended` by the transport clock when the duration is known. The rate is changed only after every pending seek has finished: asking AVPlayer to play while a seek was in flight left it frozen at the seek target in about a third of full-suite runs (player `rate 1.0`, time stuck at the target); after this change 12 consecutive full MediaEngineTests runs passed.
- **Compositor.** New optional `backgroundMedia` and `foregroundMedia` textures (stills and video frames alike). Order: background colour, background media (or, as before, the shared video), theme background image, foreground media, text, overlay images. Each media texture is drawn only if the scene names that media. When the scene names a background media, the shared `video` is not drawn, so a frame that is not ready yet shows the background colour instead of the theme loop. The foreground is aspect-fit over an opaque black quad (a 1x1 black texture), so it is opaque even on a transparent broadcast background. If the scene names a foreground but no frame is ready, nothing extra is drawn (the text is already hidden by the composer, so the audience sees the background until the first frame; a missing file therefore never blacks out the screen).
- **Outputs.** Without a `mediaSource`, `OutputRenderer` clears `backgroundMedia` / `foregroundMedia` in the scene so everything behaves as today. With one, the textures for the frame are resolved on the render thread and held by `MediaFrameHold` in the command buffer's completion handler, so the CVMetalTextures outlive the GPU work. The existing `recent` list for the shared video is unchanged.

## Not verified

- **Nothing on a real output.** No output window was shown with a background or foreground clip or still; picture, letterboxing, colour and smoothness on a projector or display are unseen. The GPU path is checked only offscreen with solid-colour textures.
- **The glue to the app is untested.** `MediaRegistry` conforming to `MediaFrameSource` and passing it to outputs happens in the app, which this slice did not touch; no test builds a real registry into a real `MetalOutput`. The two halves are tested separately (registry returns buffers; resolver and compositor consume buffers from a fake source).
- **No audio was played.** Test movies have no sound track and tests use muted players. Foreground audio on the default output, `volume`, and mute are untested. Routing to a chosen device is not implemented.
- **Performance and long runs.** No frame-rate, CPU/GPU, memory or soak measurement with video layers, no 4-output run with media, no 59.94 behaviour, no hardware output. Two players (background loop plus foreground) decoding together at 1080p was not measured; each output pulls and converts frames itself (one texture-cache conversion per layer per output per tick).
- **A/V and transport drift.** The transport clock and the player are two clocks; the player starts after its seek completes, so on a slow seek the player runs a few tens of milliseconds behind the transport. Not measured on large or long-GOP files. `MediaLayers.tick` ends a clip by the clock, the player's notification is a second signal; if a player stalls (frames not consumed, output hidden) the clock still ends it, but `setOnEnded` may not fire.
- **Only tiny synthetic H.264 files.** HEVC, ProRes, odd rotations/transforms, files with audio tracks, very long or variable-frame-rate files, animated GIFs, HEIC, and security-scoped (sandbox bookmark) access were not tried. The probe/thumbnailer handle rotation through `preferredTransform` but that is untested with a rotated file. Video `MediaProbe` of an audio-only `.caf` was tested; MP3/AAC were not.
- **Eviction/lifecycle corners.** Registry behaviour when an asset is unregistered mid-play, when a file is replaced on disk (stills are not re-read after the first decode), or when many assets are applied rapidly is lightly tested only.
- **Timing-dependent tests.** The MediaEngine tests poll for decoded frames with an 8 second ceiling; they passed on this Mac but could fail on a heavily loaded machine. I saw no failure after the rate fix (12 runs), which is not a proof of freedom from flakiness.
- **Possible test-contract notes.** I found no test in the coordinator file I believe to be wrong. Two behaviours it leaves open were decided by me and are listed under Decisions (audio ignored rather than clearing; foreground gated by the Look's slide layer).
